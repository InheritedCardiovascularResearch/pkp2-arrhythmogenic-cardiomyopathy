# PKP2-Associated Arrhythmogenic Cardiomyopathy

Research and educational resources covering PKP2, plakophilin-2, PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy, cardiac desmosomes, disease biology, clinical research, and gene therapy development.

## Overview

PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy is an inherited cardiac disease associated with pathogenic variants in the PKP2 gene, which encodes plakophilin-2.

PKP2 is an important component of the cardiac intercalated disc, where proteins involved in cell adhesion, mechanical integrity, and electrical signaling are closely organized.

Changes in PKP2 can affect both the structural organization and electrical behavior of cardiac tissue. Research has linked PKP2 dysfunction with changes in desmosomal organization, cardiac electrical activity, and susceptibility to ventricular arrhythmias.

This repository brings together information on PKP2 biology, the relationship between PKP2 and arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy, clinical research, and emerging gene therapy approaches.

The repository also covers RP-A601, an investigational gene therapy being studied for PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

## What is PKP2?

PKP2 is the gene that encodes plakophilin-2.

Plakophilin-2 is a component of the desmosome, a specialized cell junction that helps cardiac muscle cells remain mechanically connected.

In the heart, desmosomes are located within the intercalated disc, a specialized structure that connects neighboring cardiomyocytes and brings together several systems involved in cardiac tissue organization and electrical function.

PKP2 also has relationships with proteins and structures involved in cardiac electrical signaling.

This helps explain why pathogenic PKP2 variants can be associated with both structural abnormalities and arrhythmias.

PKP2 is one of the major desmosomal genes associated with arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

## What is plakophilin-2?

Plakophilin-2 is the protein produced from the PKP2 gene.

It contributes to the organization and stability of cardiac cell junctions and interacts with other proteins located at the intercalated disc.

The cardiac intercalated disc contains closely connected components of the desmosome, adherens junctions, gap junctions, and ion channel complexes.

Because these systems work together, disruption of one component can have effects beyond its primary structural role.

Research has therefore investigated PKP2 in relation to:

- Desmosomal integrity
- Intercalated-disc organization
- Cell-to-cell adhesion
- Sodium channel function
- Electrical conduction
- Cardiac excitability
- Ventricular arrhythmias
- Cardiac remodeling

## What is PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy?

PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy, commonly abbreviated PKP2-ACM, is an inherited form of arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy associated with pathogenic variants in PKP2.

Arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy is a genetically influenced cardiac disease characterized by electrical instability and structural changes in the heart.

The disease can involve ventricular arrhythmias and progressive changes in cardiac structure and function.

Historically, some patients with this disease spectrum were described as having arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy or ARVC.

Current disease concepts recognize that arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy can involve both right and left ventricular phenotypes, depending on the underlying genetic and biological factors.

PKP2 is particularly important in the desmosomal form of arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

## PKP2 and the cardiac desmosome

Desmosomes provide strong cell-to-cell adhesion.

This function is especially important in the heart because cardiomyocytes experience repeated mechanical stress as the heart contracts.

Several proteins contribute to the cardiac desmosome and surrounding intercalated-disc structure.

| Gene | Protein | General role |
|---|---|---|
| PKP2 | Plakophilin-2 | Desmosomal and intercalated-disc organization |
| DSG2 | Desmoglein-2 | Cell adhesion |
| DSC2 | Desmocollin-2 | Cell adhesion |
| DSP | Desmoplakin | Links junctional proteins to the cytoskeleton |
| JUP | Plakoglobin | Junctional and signaling functions |

These proteins form part of a larger network rather than operating independently.

For this reason, PKP2 research often examines changes in the broader intercalated-disc environment.

## Why does PKP2 matter for cardiac electrical function?

The relationship between PKP2 and arrhythmia is one of the important areas of research in arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

The intercalated disc brings together structural junctions, gap junctions, and ion channel complexes.

Research has shown that reduced PKP2 expression or disruption of PKP2 can affect the organization and function of proteins involved in cardiac electrical activity.

One area of research has focused on the relationship between PKP2 and the cardiac sodium channel complex.

Changes in this system can contribute to altered electrical conduction and increased susceptibility to arrhythmias.

This means that PKP2-associated disease cannot be understood solely as a problem of mechanical cell adhesion.

The biological picture includes interactions between:

```text
PKP2
  ↓
Plakophilin-2
  ↓
Cardiac intercalated disc
  ↓
Desmosomal organization
  ↓
Cell adhesion and electrical organization
  ↓
Cardiac electrical instability
  ↓
Arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy
```

This is a simplified representation of a much more complex biological system.

## How pathogenic PKP2 variants can affect the heart

Pathogenic PKP2 variants can reduce or alter the normal function of plakophilin-2.

The resulting changes can affect the organization of cardiac junctions and the relationship between structural and electrical components of the cardiomyocyte.

Research has associated PKP2 dysfunction with:

- Reduced desmosomal integrity
- Altered intercalated-disc organization
- Changes in sodium channel function
- Abnormal electrical conduction
- Ventricular arrhythmias
- Cardiac remodeling
- Progressive cardiac dysfunction

The clinical expression of PKP2-associated disease varies between individuals.

A genetic finding therefore does not by itself describe the full clinical course of a patient.

Clinical assessment can involve genetic testing, electrocardiography, rhythm monitoring, cardiac imaging, family history, clinical history, and other measures of cardiac function.

## PKP2-ACM and ventricular arrhythmias

Ventricular arrhythmias are an important feature of arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

One reason PKP2-ACM is an important area of research is that electrical abnormalities can become clinically relevant before extensive structural disease is necessarily apparent.

Researchers therefore study multiple indicators of disease activity and risk.

These can include:

- Ventricular tachycardia
- Ventricular arrhythmia burden
- Electrocardiographic findings
- Right ventricular function
- Left ventricular function
- Cardiac imaging
- Biomarkers
- Implantable cardioverter-defibrillator data
- Clinical history
- Genetic findings

No single measurement provides a complete description of PKP2-ACM.

Clinical research therefore combines different forms of evidence to understand disease progression and treatment response.

## Why is gene therapy being studied for PKP2-ACM?

Most established management approaches for arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy are directed toward managing the consequences and risks associated with the disease.

Gene therapy research approaches the disease from a different direction.

The underlying hypothesis is that delivering a functional copy of the affected gene could increase production of the relevant protein and potentially address part of the biological mechanism contributing to disease.

For PKP2-ACM, this means studying whether delivery of a functional PKP2 transgene can increase plakophilin-2 expression in cardiac tissue.

This is an investigational approach.

A biological rationale does not establish that a gene therapy is safe or effective in patients. Clinical studies are required to determine whether changes observed at the molecular or cellular level translate into meaningful clinical outcomes.

## RP-A601

RP-A601 is an investigational gene therapy being developed by Rocket Pharmaceuticals for PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

The program uses an adeno-associated virus-based approach to deliver a functional PKP2 transgene.

The clinical program is intended to investigate whether treatment can increase PKP2 expression and produce biological or clinical changes relevant to PKP2-ACM.

The Phase 1 clinical study is registered under:

NCT05885412

The study is evaluating RP-A601 in adults with PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

## RP-A601 Phase 1 study

The Phase 1 study is an early clinical investigation.

The study evaluates areas including:

- Safety
- Tolerability
- PKP2 myocardial protein expression
- Cardiac biomarkers
- Arrhythmia-related measures
- Cardiac function
- Clinical measures associated with ventricular arrhythmia risk

The study is important because it allows researchers to examine whether the biological rationale for PKP2 gene replacement can be observed in patients.

Early clinical development does not establish long-term efficacy.

The purpose of Phase 1 development is to generate information that can guide subsequent clinical development and determine whether a therapeutic approach warrants further investigation.

## What has been reported from RP-A601?

Rocket Pharmaceuticals has reported preliminary clinical findings from the Phase 1 study.

The company reported increased PKP2 protein expression in treated patients and improved desmosomal localization of PKP2 and associated proteins.

The company also reported directional improvements in certain arrhythmia measures and right ventricular function.

These findings provide early biological and clinical information about the investigational approach.

They should be interpreted in the context of the early stage of development and the number of patients included in the reported dataset.

Preliminary findings are not equivalent to definitive evidence of clinical efficacy.

Future data and longer follow-up are important for determining whether observed biological changes translate into durable clinical benefit.

## Current development status of RP-A601

As of the latest publicly available company update reviewed for this repository, the RP-A601 Phase 1 study remains open and enrolling.

Rocket Pharmaceuticals has also reported continued engagement with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration concerning the design of a potential pivotal Phase 2 study.

This distinction is important.

RP-A601 is not an approved treatment for PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

A potential pivotal study represents a future stage of development and should not be described as an ongoing pivotal trial unless an official source confirms that status.

The current status of the clinical trial should be checked against the latest ClinicalTrials.gov record and subsequent company disclosures.

## From Phase 1 to potential pivotal development

Clinical development progresses through stages because different stages answer different questions.

A Phase 1 study can provide information about:

- Safety
- Tolerability
- Dose
- Biological activity
- Initial clinical observations

A later-stage pivotal study has a different purpose.

Its design must provide a sufficiently rigorous framework for evaluating the safety and clinical benefit of a therapy in a defined patient population.

The transition from Phase 1 to potential pivotal development therefore involves questions such as:

- Which patients should be included?
- What evidence from Phase 1 supports continued development?
- Which biological measurements are meaningful?
- Which clinical endpoints should be evaluated?
- How long should patients be followed?
- What safety monitoring is required?
- What statistical design is appropriate?
- What evidence will be relevant to regulatory review?

For RP-A601, these questions form part of the ongoing development and regulatory discussions surrounding potential pivotal development.

## Key research questions

PKP2-ACM gene therapy research is addressing several important questions.

### Can PKP2 expression be restored?

One central question is whether gene delivery can increase functional PKP2 protein expression in cardiac tissue.

### Does increased PKP2 improve cellular organization?

Researchers are examining whether changes in PKP2 expression are associated with improved organization of proteins within the cardiac intercalated disc.

### Can biological changes affect arrhythmia?

A key translational question is whether molecular and cellular changes can produce meaningful changes in ventricular arrhythmia burden or other clinical measures.

### Which patients are most likely to benefit?

PKP2-ACM has variable clinical expression. Patient selection may therefore become an important consideration as clinical development advances.

### What evidence is needed for later-stage development?

The transition from an early clinical study to a potential pivotal study requires decisions about patient population, endpoints, follow-up, statistical design, and safety.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does PKP2 stand for?

PKP2 is the gene that encodes plakophilin-2.

### What is plakophilin-2?

Plakophilin-2 is a protein associated with cardiac desmosomes and the intercalated disc. It contributes to the organization of cardiac cell junctions and has relationships with systems involved in electrical signaling.

### What is PKP2-ACM?

PKP2-ACM is an inherited form of arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy associated with pathogenic variants in PKP2.

### Is PKP2-ACM the same as ARVC?

PKP2-ACM overlaps substantially with the disease historically described as arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy, or ARVC. Modern descriptions of arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy recognize a broader range of cardiac phenotypes.

### Why can PKP2 variants cause arrhythmias?

PKP2 is involved in the organization of the cardiac intercalated disc and interacts with proteins involved in electrical signaling. PKP2 dysfunction can therefore affect both structural and electrical properties of cardiac tissue.

### What is RP-A601?

RP-A601 is an investigational gene therapy being studied for PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

### Who is developing RP-A601?

RP-A601 is being developed by Rocket Pharmaceuticals.

### What clinical trial is studying RP-A601?

The Phase 1 clinical study is registered as NCT05885412.

### Is RP-A601 approved?

No. RP-A601 is an investigational therapy and is not an approved treatment for PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

### What has been reported from the RP-A601 clinical program?

Rocket Pharmaceuticals has reported preliminary findings including increased PKP2 protein expression, improved desmosomal localization, and directional changes in selected arrhythmia and right ventricular measures.

These findings remain part of an early-stage clinical development program.

### What does RMAT designation mean?

Regenerative Medicine Advanced Therapy designation is an FDA designation intended to facilitate development and review of certain regenerative medicine therapies for serious or life-threatening diseases.

RMAT designation does not mean that a therapy has received FDA approval.
